# Personal Finance Step 5 specification notes

Source: /home/ubuntu/upload/pasted_content.txt

Step 5 remodels Personal Finance into a simple active budgeting assistant. Preserve Trips, Shared Home, Payment, UPI, Cash, Member, and other existing functionality. Keep primary navigation simple: Overview, Income, Expenses; do not create unnecessary top-level tabs. Recurring expenses remain managed inside Expenses.

Expenses support three entry choices: Daily Expense, Weekly Expense, and Other / One-Time Expense. Daily entry is compact with amount, quick category, date defaulting to today, optional person/payment method/note, numeric amount keyboard, and quick actions such as Milk, Food, Transport, and Other. Weekly entry stores one combined amount for a named week/category and must not create duplicate daily transactions. Daily and weekly records remain distinct.

Expense dashboard shows planned/recurring, daily, weekly, unexpected, save/invest, liabilities, and total cash outflow. Remaining Available Cash is expected income minus mandatory recurring commitments, expected variable expenses, daily/weekly spending, unexpected expenses, liability payments, and savings/goal allocations. Safe Spending Amount and Suggested Daily Spending Limit divide remaining discretionary cash by remaining month days. Guidance is advisory only and never moves money automatically.

Smart Budget Guidance uses Information, Attention, and Warning levels, neutral language, no shame, and at most 2–3 prominent messages. Priorities are critical budget overrun, goal significantly behind, upcoming major commitment, high daily spending, and minor category variance. Guidance includes low remaining budget, high daily spending, goal shortfall, category overspending, weekly above/below target, and positive progress.

Goals are accessed from Overview or Expenses → Save/Invest → Goals, not as a separate top-level tab. Goal fields include name, target amount, current saved amount, target date or years, priority Low/Medium/High, monthly required/target allocation, active state, notes. Required monthly allocation is remaining goal amount divided by remaining months. Users can add Goal Contributions with amount/date/source/note; contributions reduce available cash and increase the goal fund but must not inflate normal spending totals. Suggested monthly allocation is guidance/planned budget only; no automatic transfer. Multiple goals show total monthly goal commitment. Goal affordability warnings offer review spending, adjust goal, reduce allocation, or extend date without automatically changing the goal.

Overview should show Money In, Committed, Goals, Actual Spending, Unexpected, Remaining, Goal Progress, Budget Guidance, Saved/Invested, savings rate, and spending status. The conceptual calculation order is expected income, mandatory recurring commitments, liabilities, required goal allocation, variable expense budget, daily/weekly discretionary spending, remaining cash, financial progress. Avoid accounting jargon and double counting between recurring, daily, weekly, goal contributions, liabilities, and unexpected expenses.

Acceptance examples include daily Food ₹500 and Transport ₹300 immediately reducing remaining discretionary cash; Weekly Groceries ₹4,000 increasing monthly spending once without daily duplicates; Emergency Fund ₹5,00,000 target with ₹1,00,000 current over 40 months requiring ₹10,000/month; a ₹5,000 contribution increasing goal current amount and reducing cash without increasing spending; warnings for remaining cash, daily limit, and goal shortfall. Tests must cover calculations and the production build.
